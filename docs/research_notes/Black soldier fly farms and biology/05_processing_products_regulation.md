# Black Soldier Fly (Hermetia illucens): Post-Harvest Processing, Product Quality, Nutrition, Markets and Regulation (focus: whole dried larvae)

Research notes compiled Sept 2026. Unless stated, composition figures are dry-matter (DM) basis when the source says so; "as-fed" is flagged explicitly. Items marked "(search snippet)" come from search-engine abstracts and not from the full text, so they carry lower confidence. Items marked "(industry/retailer claim)" are commercial and potentially biased.

---

## 1. Killing methods: effects on microbes, colour, lipid oxidation, nutrients and welfare

### Takeaway
Blanching (boiling water for about 40 s) is the best-supported killing step. It gives the lowest microbial load, destroys lipase and gives the lowest secondary oxidation. It is fast, which is why welfare researchers accept it. Slow heating of live larvae (oven "desiccation") is worse for microbes, oxidation and welfare. Asphyxiation and grinding are the worst for quality.

### Cited Findings
- Larouche et al. (2019, *Animals* 9:182) compared 10 killing methods. After killing, all samples were freeze-dried before analysis. The methods were:
  - blanching: boiling water, 40 s
  - desiccation: 60 °C air oven, 30 min
  - freezing: −20 °C or −40 °C for 1 h
  - liquid nitrogen: 40 s
  - asphyxiation at 27 °C in the dark: CO₂ (120 h), N₂ (144 h) or vacuum (120 h)
  - grinding: 15,000 rpm, 2 min
  - high hydrostatic pressure (HHP): 600 MPa, 3 min

  — [Larouche et al. 2019, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/)
- Microbial load after killing (log CFU/g) — [Larouche et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/):

  | Killing method | Total viable count | Enterobacteriaceae | Yeasts/moulds |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Blanching | 5.6 | <1.1 | <2.1 |
  | HHP | 6.3 | <1.1 | <2.1 |
  | Desiccation, 60 °C 30 min | 7.7 | 4.1 | 5.1 |
  | Freezing −20 °C | 8.3 | 4.5 | 6.3 |
  | Freezing −40 °C | 8.5 | 5.3 | 6.4 |
  | Grinding | 8.5 | 4.7 | 6.1 |
  | CO₂ asphyxiation | 9.8 | 7.5 | 5.8 |
  | N₂ asphyxiation | 9.6 | 7.3 | 5.3 |
  | Vacuum | 9.6 | 6.9 | 4.2 |

- Even after blanching, the total viable count of 5.6 log was about 1.6 log above the Health Canada guideline of <4 log for ready-to-eat foods. Listeria spp. and clostridia were also detected, so the authors say further decontamination (for example the later drying step) is needed — [Larouche et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/)
- Lipid oxidation after blanching was the lowest among the methods:
  - Abstract values: primary oxidation 4.6 ± 0.7 mg cumene hydroperoxide eq/kg; secondary (TBARS) 1.0 ± 0.1 mg MDA/kg; blanching started dehydration, giving water content 78.1 ± 1.0% — [Larouche et al. 2019, abstract](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-2615/9/4/182)
  - Full-text table values (as extracted): primary oxidation (FOX) was about 6–8 mg CHP/kg for blanching, desiccation, freezing, grinding and HHP, but 18.6–19.4 for the asphyxiation methods; TBARS was 1.0 for blanching versus 2.5 for desiccation and 1.6–2.0 for the other methods — [Larouche et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/)
  - The two sets of blanching figures differ slightly (4.6 vs 6.4 for primary oxidation). Treat the abstract as authoritative.
- Blanching gave pH 8.7, versus 6.1–6.3 after asphyxiation and 7.3–7.4 after freezing — [Larouche et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/)
- Colour change (ΔE):
  - Grinding caused by far the largest colour change: ΔE 17.5 and the darkest product (L* 37.2).
  - Blanching gave ΔE 6.2 (L* 39.9, b* 9.7).
  - Asphyxiation gave ΔE 2.2.

  — [Larouche et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/)
- Blanching destroys lipase and so minimises harmful reactions such as lipid oxidation. Freezing only reduces lipase activity and can lead to rancid fat and lower pH — [Larouche 2019 via Laval summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339537156_Processing_methods_for_the_black_soldier_fly_Hermetia_illucens_larvae_From_feed_withdrawal_periods_to_killing_methods) (search snippet)
- A 2026 review reports that blanching (100 °C, 40 s) cut microbial load by 3–5 log CFU/g and inhibited lipase — [Eur Food Res Technol 2026 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00217-026-05050-7) (search snippet)
- Heat-based killing such as blanching gave less enzymatic and non-enzymatic browning, and less darkening, than other killing procedures — [Applied Sciences 2020, "Impacts of Killing Process…"](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/10/17/6099) (search snippet; the attribution to this exact paper was not full-text verified)
- A study of slaughter × drying × defatting used blanching at 90 °C for 40 s (larvae:water 1:10 w/v) and freezing at −20 °C for 24 h. For the extracted fat:
  - The best peroxide-value stability over 24 weeks came from freezing + freeze-drying + mechanical pressing.
  - The worst initial peroxide value (11.7 mEq O₂/kg) came from blanching + oven-drying (65 °C, 24 h) + hexane extraction.
  - Blanching + oven-drying + mechanical pressing gave the longest Rancimat induction time (23.9 h).

  — [Foods 2023, PMC10142283](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10142283/)
- On welfare, Barrett et al. (2023) list the slaughter methods used for BSF larvae: freezing (air or liquid N₂), baking in a convection oven, roasting in sand or sunshine, microwaving, boiling/blanching, asphyxiation and grinding/shredding. They define humane slaughter as killing the animal instantly or making it insensible until death "without pain, suffering or distress". They also judge last-instar dipteran larvae to have "substantial evidence for sentience" — [Barrett et al. 2023, JIFF 9(2)](https://dx.doi.org/10.3920/JIFF2022.0041); [search summary](https://rethinkpriorities.org/research-area/welfare-considerations-for-farmed-black-soldier-flies-hermetia-illucens/)
- Larouche et al. consider blanching "an acceptable way to kill insects because of its rapidity" — [Larouche et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/)
- Grinding can be humane only if killing is instantaneous. There is a dedicated empirical paper on the grinder settings that achieve this — [Animal Welfare, PMC10951668](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10951668/) (title only; parameters not extracted)
- Holding killed larvae before drying:
  - Blanching (a decontamination step) greatly lowers the starting microbial load.
  - Killed, blanched larvae needed 4 °C (not 15 °C) to keep acceptable counts after 6 days.
  - Vacuum packing gave no meaningful benefit for either live or killed larvae.

  — [SUSINCHAIN best-practice sheet, KU Leuven](https://susinchain.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Best-practice-Storage-and-transport-of-BSF-larvae.pdf)

### Inferences
- Tryento puts live larvae straight into the oven. This is close to Larouche's "desiccation" treatment (60 °C for 30 min), which left about 2 log more total viable count and about 3 log more Enterobacteriaceae than blanching, and 2.5× the TBARS of blanching. It is also the slow-heating "baking" route that welfare reviewers rank poorly. Adding a 40 s boiling-water blanch (then draining or spinning off surface water) is the single change best supported by evidence on all four criteria: microbes, oxidation, colour and welfare.
- Blanching raises pH to about 8.7 and inactivates lipase and, probably, phenoloxidase. Phenoloxidase inactivation is an inference: the fetched text reports less browning but does not name the enzyme. This should also help produce a consistent golden colour instead of grey or dark patches. Grinding or crushing larvae before heating gave the darkest product, which is consistent with enzymatic browning or melanisation when tissue is damaged.
- If blanched larvae cannot go into the oven the same day, hold them at ≤4 °C. Do not hold them at ambient temperature.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the exact welfare ranking table in Barrett et al. 2023, including whether oven-baking live larvae ranks last. Only the list of methods and the definitions were confirmed.
- Microwave killing of live larvae was not covered separately in the killing studies I retrieved. Nutrient-retention differences between killing methods (amino acids, vitamins) were not extracted. The Applied Sciences 2020 paper covers this but was not read in full.

## 2. Drying: methods, time/temperature, target moisture/aw, yield, energy, tray loading, golden and crunchy product

### Takeaway
Fresh larvae are about 70–77% water, with aw ≈ 0.9. Dry to aw <0.6 (and ≤10% moisture, the AAFCO definition). Expect a dry/wet yield of 25–35%; a yield above 35% means drying is not finished.

- Slow oven drying at about 65 °C gives a hard, stiff, darker, denser product.
- Fast, high-temperature methods (microwave, sand/drum roasting at ≤150–180 °C product temperature) give the puffed, crispy, yellowish product that sells at a premium.

### Cited Findings
- Moisture, aw and composition before and after drying:
  - Freshly harvested BSFL are about 70% water and about 30% dry matter, with aw ≈ 0.9.
  - aw <0.6 inhibits the growth of all bacteria and yeasts.
  - In a fruit-waste reference batch, fresh larvae had 76.5 ± 3% moisture and aw 0.9. Dried larvae had 2.6 ± 0.8% moisture, aw 0.4 ± 0.1, 36.4% protein, 36.2% fat, 13.5% fibre, 7.0% ash and 6.2% carbohydrate (as-is).

  — [Eawag/Sandec factsheet "Small-scale drying methods for BSFL"](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- Yield (dry mass ÷ fresh mass) "lies typically between 25–35%". The factsheet says: "an output yield higher than 35% implies that the drying process is not yet completed". Every method's step-by-step manual ends with a check that yield falls within 25–35% — [Eawag factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- Texture mechanism: rapid dehydration builds steam pressure inside the larva until it releases, giving "puffed and crispy larvae". Slow dehydration shrinks the shell steadily, giving a less voluminous, "hard and stiff" product. The puffed product "is more appealing to customers and thus has a higher market value", especially in pet food — [Eawag factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- The Eawag table of four small-scale methods — [Eawag factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf):

  | Parameter | Microwave | Sand roasting, pan | Sand roasting, drum | Oven |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Product | crispy, puffed, yellowish | crispy, puffed, yellowish/brown | crispy, puffed, yellowish/brown | hard, stiff, **dark brown** |
  | Bulk density | 110–130 g/L | 110–130 g/L | 110–130 g/L | 220–230 g/L |
  | Max product temperature | 180 °C | 180 °C | 150 °C | 65 °C |
  | Batch | 0.25 kg | 0.7 kg | 4 kg | 30 kg |
  | Time | 15 min | 15 min | 30 min | 24 h |
  | Throughput | 0.9 kg/h | 2.5 kg/h | 6.2 kg/h | 1.2 kg/h |
  | Energy per kg dried larvae | "3,700 Watt" (units as printed; probably Wh) | 0.9 kg LPG | 0.7 kg LPG | 0.7 kg LPG |
  | Labour | high | high | medium | low |

- The oven method in detail:
  - Larvae "have to be killed before the process, e.g. by rapid boiling".
  - Dry at 65 °C with a fan.
  - Load 3 kg of freshly boiled larvae on each of 10 mesh trays (65 × 45 × 3.5 cm, 4 mm mesh).
  - Dry in one 16 h night phase plus two 4 h day phases, shuffling tray positions between phases on a fixed rotation.
  - The end point is a "hard and dry texture, shrunken shape".

  — [Eawag factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- The microwave method in detail: 250 g of fresh larvae, 3 cycles of 5 min at 1000 W, opening the door for 20 s between cycles to vent steam. Excess steam gives "damp and non-puffed larvae". Add a fourth cycle of 2–3 min if the larvae are not yet crispy and puffed — [Eawag factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- The sand-roasting method in detail: preheat 700 g of sand (0.8–1.5 mm) to about 200 °C, add 700 g of fresh larvae and stir continuously for about 15 min, then sieve out the sand. Sand-roasted larvae are "a bit darker" than microwaved ones — [Eawag factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- Larvae for fish or chicken feed must be dried to 8–10% moisture for milling and storage. A reversible-airflow flatbed dryer at 70 °C took 12.8 h to bring larvae from 73.0% to 8.6% moisture — [ResearchGate 2025, drying BSFL for fish/poultry feed](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390743319_Experimental_study_on_drying_black_soldier_fly_larvae_used_in_the_production_of_fish_and_poultry_feed) (search snippet)
- Thin-layer drying at 40–70 °C followed the Page model, with moisture diffusivity 6.15 × 10⁻¹¹ to 2.63 × 10⁻¹⁰ m²/s and activation energy 48.66 kJ/mol. Sorption isotherms were measured at 40–70 °C — [Food Biophysics 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11483-024-09867-1) (search snippet)
- One pig-feed study compared four drying methods: hot air (65 °C, 24 h), microwave (700 W, 5 min × 3), freeze-drying (−40 °C, 72 h) and infrared (95 °C, 12 min). In-vitro nutrient digestibility of full-fat larvae was "fairly comparable". Microwave-dried material had higher free fatty acids, acid value and iodine value than oven-dried material — [Agriculture 2024, 14:1792](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture14101792) (search snippet)
- Dielectric drying (microwave and radio frequency):
  - It cut aw effectively.
  - It retained or improved microbiological quality.
  - It is described as a more efficient alternative to oven and freeze drying in time and energy.

  — [JIFF 2025, 11(17)](https://brill.com/view/journals/jiff/11/17/article-p21_4.xml) (search snippet; full text blocked)
- Variable-power microwave drying cut drying time by 17.1–27.5% and energy by 3.4–12.2% compared with constant-power microwave drying — [Innov. Food Sci. Emerg. Technol. 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1466856423002047) (search snippet)
- One equipment vendor says conventional rotary or forced-air dryers use 5–10 kWh of electricity per kg of dried larvae — [Kerone](https://kerone.com/microwave-drying-revolutionizes-black-soldier-fly-bsf-processing/) (industry claim from a microwave-dryer seller; treat as biased)
- Under the AAFCO definition, dried BSFL is "dried by artificial means to no more than 10% moisture" — [AAFCO IDC minutes, Jan 2025](https://www.aafco.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IDC_Minutes_MY_2025.pdf)
- For long-term storage, dried insects should be microbiologically stable, and "a water activity below 0.60 is advised" — [SUSINCHAIN](https://susinchain.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Best-practice-Storage-and-transport-of-BSF-larvae.pdf)
- Brazil's MAPA requires processors to document drying in their SOPs:
  - the dryer type and how it operates
  - minimum process parameters, with measuring equipment, monitoring and verification
  - "studies showing that the dehydration temperature and time are applied uniformly throughout the equipment"

  — [MAPA Ofício-Circular 33/2023](https://wikisda.agricultura.gov.br/manual_fiscalizacao_aa/manual_autocontrole_aa/circ_2023-33_aa_processamento_de_insetos.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Benchmarking Tryento's rendimiento.** Yield = (1 − fresh moisture) ÷ (1 − final moisture).
  - With 76.5% fresh moisture: a final moisture of 5% gives about 24.7% yield; 10% gives about 26.1%.
  - With 70% fresh moisture: 5% final gives about 31.6%; 10% final gives about 33.3%.
  - So:
    - A lot above about 35% is almost certainly under-dried (Eawag's own rule).
    - A lot below about 22% suggests either very wet fresh larvae (for example water carried over from blanching or washing) or fat and mass lost through over-roasting.
  - Once the farm measures fresh-larva moisture now and then (oven-dry a small sample at 105 °C to constant weight), it can back-calculate final moisture for each lote from the wet and dry weights it already records.
- **Tray loading.** Eawag's reference loading of 3 kg per 0.2925 m² tray is about 10 kg of fresh larvae per m² in a single layer on mesh. Deeper layers or solid trays will lengthen drying and make it uneven. Since Tryento uses solid metal trays, it should either shuffle tray positions (as in the Eawag rotation) or turn or stir the larvae.
- **Golden and crunchy.** The evidence suggests that a golden, puffed, crunchy product needs fast, high-intensity heating: microwave cycles with steam venting, or sand/drum roasting with product temperature ≤150–180 °C.
  - Low-temperature oven drying (about 65 °C, 24 h) gives a dark-brown, hard, dense product.
  - A practical hybrid that has not been tested in the literature: blanch → partially oven-dry → short high-temperature finish (roast or microwave) to puff and crisp. Watch colour to stop before "tostado" (scorched).
  - Scorching risk rises with product temperatures above about 180 °C, and with thick, unstirred layers in contact with hot metal.
- **Linking crunch grades to moisture.** No study was found that links sensory crunch grades to moisture. A reasonable hypothesis to check in-house:
  - "blando" ≈ incomplete drying (moisture probably >10%, aw >0.6)
  - "muy crujiente" ≈ moisture of a few % with aw ≈ 0.3–0.5 (Eawag's crispy product was 2.6% moisture, aw 0.4)
  - "tostado" = colour and burnt odour past the target, not a moisture state

  Calibrate this by measuring moisture and aw on graded samples.
- **Energy.** 0.7 kg of LPG per kg of dried larvae in the oven is about 9 kWh of fuel per kg. This assumes about 46 MJ/kg for LPG, which is my assumption and not from the source. If the "3,700 W" figure means Wh, a kitchen microwave uses about 3.7 kWh of electricity per kg.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that links graded crunchiness or golden colour to moisture or aw for whole BSFL. The farm will need to calibrate this itself.
- Figures not obtained:
  - fluidised-bed drying parameters for BSFL
  - solar or sun drying times and final moisture for BSFL
  - peer-reviewed energy use (kWh/kg) for convective ovens
- The full-text colour (L*a*b*) versus drying-temperature data were not accessible because MDPI and Springer blocked access.

## 3. Quality control: objective small-farm tests and microbial criteria

### Takeaway
Practical QC for a small farm:
- the lote mass balance (yield within 25–35%)
- a moisture target of ≤10% (AAFCO), preferably much lower for crunch
- aw <0.6 (ideally about 0.4)
- sensory checks: colour, crunch, rancid odour
- occasional laboratory tests: Salmonella and Enterobacteriaceae (EU criteria) and peroxide value

The EU Regulation 142/2011 Annex X criteria are the clearest numerical microbial standard to adopt.

### Cited Findings
- **EU insect processed animal protein (PAP), Reg. (EU) 142/2011 Annex X.** Samples of the final product, taken during storage or when leaving storage at the processing plant, must meet:
  - *Salmonella*: absent in 25 g
  - Enterobacteriaceae: n = 5, c = 2, m = 10, M = 300 CFU/g

  — [Reg. 142/2011 Annex X](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/142/annex/X); [Animal (2025) review of EU insect legislation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1751731125000515) (search snippet)
- **EU processing methods.** Insect PAP must be produced with one of the processing methods in 142/2011 (Annex IV Chapter III). Alternatively, method 7 can be authorised nationally after a hazard analysis. For method 7, the final product must be sampled over 30 days for *Salmonella*, Enterobacteriaceae and *Clostridium perfringens* — [Animal 2025 review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1751731125000515) (search snippet)
- **Health Canada.** The guideline for ready-to-eat foods is <4 log CFU/g total viable count. Blanched larvae at 5.6 log did not meet it before drying — [Larouche et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523926/)
- **Frass (EU Reg. 2021/1925).** Frass must be heat-treated at ≥70 °C for ≥60 min. Straight after treatment, *E. coli* or Enterococcaceae must be below 3.0 log CFU/g. *Salmonella* must be absent during storage or when frass leaves storage — [Frass heat-treatment study, PMC12120769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12120769/) (search snippet)
- **Brazil's MAPA SOP requirements** — [MAPA Ofício-Circular 33/2023](https://wikisda.agricultura.gov.br/manual_fiscalizacao_aa/manual_autocontrole_aa/circ_2023-33_aa_processamento_de_insetos.pdf):
  - Receiving larvae: check for dead or decomposing larvae and atypical odour or colour.
  - Separating larvae from substrate and frass: specify the technique and how often it is monitored.
  - Cooking step: temperature range, time, monitoring and measuring equipment.
  - Drying step: minimum parameters and proof that heat is applied uniformly.
  - Finished product: microbiological specifications "based on a literature reference or international legislation".
  - Storage: temperature monitoring where applicable.
  - Suppliers: keep supplier qualification records.
  - Substrate: specify type, origin, receiving specification, pre-treatment, storage and quality control for physical, chemical and microbiological hazards.
- **Oxidation measures.** Primary oxidation is measured as peroxide value (meq O₂/kg fat) and secondary oxidation as p-anisidine value. Both were used to monitor dried BSFL under nitrogen — [SUSINCHAIN](https://susinchain.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Best-practice-Storage-and-transport-of-BSF-larvae.pdf)
- **Moisture and labelling in the US.** The AAFCO definition requires ≤10% moisture and a label guarantee of minimum crude protein and minimum crude fat on an as-fed basis — [AAFCO IDC minutes Jan 2025](https://www.aafco.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IDC_Minutes_MY_2025.pdf)
- **Colombia.** A Colombian study checked BSF larva meal against ICA microbiological criteria from 1996 and 1999. It tested aerobic mesophiles, total coliforms, *E. coli*, *Clostridium*, moulds, yeasts and *Salmonella*, and found counts below ICA limits — [Rev. Fac. Nal. Agr. Medellín 2004 (Angelópolis, Antioquia)](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0304-28472004000200009) (search snippet)

### Inferences
Suggested QC tiers for Tryento, based on the sources above:

- **Every lote:**
  - Yield % within 25–35%, plus a back-calculated final moisture.
  - Colour against a printed reference card with photos of acceptable golden versus tostado.
  - Crunch test: snap and crush between the fingers, or a standard bite by two graders.
  - Smell: no rancid, sour or ammonia odour.
  - Visual check for sand, frass, mould and dead-grey larvae.
- **Every lote, if affordable:**
  - Moisture by a halogen moisture analyser (about 105 °C) on a ground sample; target ≤10%, and probably ≤5% for a "muy crujiente" grade.
  - aw meter reading, target <0.60.
- **Periodically (monthly or quarterly), external laboratory:**
  - *Salmonella* in 25 g and Enterobacteriaceae (EU limits as the benchmark)
  - total plate count, and moulds and yeasts
  - peroxide value on extracted fat at 0, 90 and 180 days of storage
- **When a new substrate is introduced:** heavy metals (Cd, Pb) in the larvae.
- The farm's aprobado/rechazado decision could be tied to objective cut-offs (yield, aw, colour card) as well as the crunch grade.

### Gaps
- I did not find a regulatory peroxide-value limit specific to dried insects. SUSINCHAIN refers to "international threshold values" set for fish oils (Ismail et al. 2016), but the numbers were not captured.
- Mould and yeast, and total plate count, limits specific to insect feed were not found in EU law (only *Salmonella* and Enterobacteriaceae are set). The US FDA *Salmonella* policy for animal feed and pet food was not retrieved.
- The limits in Venezuela's COVENIN standards for animal feed microbiology were not found.

## 4. Shelf life and packaging versus the 180-day default best-before

### Takeaway
Once aw is below 0.6, dried larvae are microbiologically stable. Shelf life is then limited by oxidation of their fat, which is typically 30–36% of the dried product. Evidence supports about 6 months in good containers at around 24 °C. Nitrogen flushing or freezing strongly suppresses oxidation. The 180-day default is plausible but has not been validated for hot tropical storage or for thin, oxygen-permeable bags.

### Cited Findings
- **Container type (Kamau et al. 2018).** Dried, ground BSFL was stored for 6 months, ambient and refrigerated, in woven polypropylene sacks, polyethylene bags and screw-lid plastic containers. At ambient conditions (23.6 ± 2.7 °C, 57.6 ± 7.0% RH), chemical deterioration ran 1.5× faster in polypropylene and 1.2× faster in polyethylene than in screw-lid containers — [Kamau et al. 2018, PubMed 29579943](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29579943/); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322396471_Moisture_adsorption_properties_and_shelf-life_estimation_of_dried_and_pulverised_edible_house_cricket_Acheta_domesticus_L_and_black_soldier_fly_larvae_Hermetia_illucens_L) (search snippet)
- **Nitrogen versus freezing.** Blanched, microwave-dried BSFL packed under 100% N₂ and held at 40 °C for 3 days kept primary and secondary oxidation "(far) below international threshold values". Frozen air-packed controls also stayed low. N₂ flushing "may thus serve as a more cost-efficient way to preserve dried insects compared to freezing" — [SUSINCHAIN](https://susinchain.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Best-practice-Storage-and-transport-of-BSF-larvae.pdf)
- **Vacuum packing** gave no advantage for live or killed (undried) larvae and is labelled a practice to avoid for those. It was not tested on dried larvae — [SUSINCHAIN](https://susinchain.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Best-practice-Storage-and-transport-of-BSF-larvae.pdf); [Van Campenhout et al. 2021, Insects 12:714](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8396865/)
- **Fat stability over 24 weeks.** Fat from larvae that were frozen, freeze-dried and mechanically pressed showed "essentially no change" in peroxide value over 24 weeks. Accelerated (Rancimat) stability was higher for oven-dried than for freeze-dried material (15.9 h vs 4.6 h), which the authors link to free fatty acids and preserved antioxidants — [Foods 2023, PMC10142283](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10142283/)
- **Microwave drying** increased free fatty acids, acid value and iodine value compared with oven drying — [Agriculture 2024, 14:1792](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture14101792) (search snippet)
- **Retailer storage claims** (commercial, unverified):
  - Dried BSFL "will last up to 6 months with no degradation" in a dark, cool cabinet with a tight lid, or up to a year frozen or refrigerated — [Rainbow Mealworms](https://www.rainbowmealworms.net/dehdrated-black-soldier-fly-larvae/)
  - Another US seller claims "up to two years" in a cool, dry place — [Mealworms by the Pound](https://www.mealwormsbythepound.com/products/dried-mealworms-11-lb-wpz8j-6pe3f)

### Inferences
- **180 days is reasonable** if all of these hold:
  - aw is below about 0.5 at packing
  - the bag is a high-barrier laminate (for example PET/Al/PE or metallised film), not plain PE or woven PP
  - headspace oxygen is removed with an N₂ flush or an oxygen absorber sachet
  - stock is kept cool and out of light

  At Venezuelan ambient temperatures of 28–35 °C and thin PE bags, oxidation will run faster than in Kamau's roughly 24 °C trial. There, 180 days is not supported and 90–120 days would be more cautious.
- **Validate the date in-house.** Keep retained samples from each lote and test peroxide value, p-anisidine value and smell at 0, 90 and 180 days, plus an accelerated test at about 40 °C. Keep 180 days only if the fat stays below the farm's chosen limit.
- Blanching before drying inactivates lipase and should limit the build-up of free fatty acids during storage.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed shelf-life study of *whole* (not ground) full-fat dried BSFL at tropical temperatures, and none testing oxygen absorbers specifically. Whole larvae may oxidise more slowly than meal because less surface is exposed, but this is unverified.
- I found no source that gives a scientific basis for a 180-day best-before specifically.

## 5. Nutritional composition and effects of substrate and harvest stage

### Takeaway
Dried BSFL is roughly 36–44% crude protein and 22–37% fat on a DM basis. It is exceptionally high in calcium (up to 5–8% DM on some substrates, giving a very high Ca:P ratio), lysine-rich (about 5–8% of protein) and dominated by lauric acid (C12:0).

Fat and ash, and to a lesser extent protein, depend strongly on substrate. Oil-rich or vegetable substrates raise fat; digestate or manure lowers fat and raises ash.

### Cited Findings
- **Spranghers et al. 2017, substrate comparison.** Prepupae reared on chicken feed, vegetable waste, biogas digestate or restaurant waste:
  - crude protein 399–431 g/kg DM
  - amino-acid profiles differed only slightly
  - ether extract and ash differed "substantially": digestate gave 218 g/kg DM fat and 197 g/kg DM ash, versus 371 g/kg DM fat and 96 g/kg DM ash on vegetable waste

  — [Spranghers et al. 2017, J Sci Food Agric 97:2594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27734508/)
- **Feedipedia overview** — [Feedipedia BSF datasheet](https://www.feedipedia.org/node/16388):
  - crude protein about 40–44% DM
  - ether extract 15–25% DM on poultry manure, 28% on swine manure, 35% on cattle manure and 42–49% on oil-rich food waste
  - ash 11–28% DM
  - Ca 5–8% DM
  - P 0.6–1.5% DM
  - lysine 6–8% of protein
  - lauric acid 21% of fatty acids on cow manure versus 43% on fish offal plus cow manure
- **2026 aquaculture review, Table 1 ranges** — [Frontiers in Aquaculture 2026 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aquaculture/articles/10.3389/faquc.2026.1862744/full):
  - crude protein 26–47%, lipid 18–48%, crude fibre 3.6–16.2%, ash 2.7–15.5%
  - amino acids (mg/g protein; Fuso et al. 2021): leucine 67–89, lysine 47–72, methionine + cysteine 32–45, tryptophan 7–19, valine 56–72
  - chitin in the exoskeleton "can hinder nutrient uptake" and limits high inclusion rates
- **Commercial whole dried larvae (4th–5th instar) used in a cat trial**, DM basis: crude protein 42%, crude fat 36.2%, Ca 1.49%, P 0.81%, lauric acid 12.74% (basis as reported) — [PMC12023938](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12023938/)
- **Whole dehydrated BSFL (Entomo Consulting) used in a laying-hen trial**, as-fed: 34.9% crude protein, 30.3% ether extract, 2.20% lysine, 1.69% methionine (as reported) — [PMC11394493](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11394493/)
- **Fruit-waste-reared larvae:** fresh larvae 10.2% protein and 7.2% fat as-is; dried larvae (2.6% moisture) 36.4% protein, 36.2% fat, 13.5% fibre and 7.0% ash — [Eawag factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- **Venezuelan data (IVIC, August 2024).** Pupae reared on IVIC cafeteria waste had 56.06% protein (basis not stated). The life cycle took 71 days under their conditions, versus about 35 days at ideal temperatures of 22–30 °C — [IVIC via Telecom (gob.ve)](https://telecom.gob.ve/resultados-de-estudio-con-mosca-soldado-negra-indican-que-es-un-potencial-para-alimento-balanceado-de-animales/)
- **US commercial label** (imported product): crude protein ≥30%, crude fat ≥33%, fibre ≤8%, moisture ≤10% (as-fed) — [Mealworms by the Pound](https://www.mealwormsbythepound.com/products/dried-mealworms-11-lb-wpz8j-6pe3f)
- **Marketing claim:** a US treat brand claims "50x more calcium than mealworms" — [Tractor Supply listing](https://www.tractorsupply.com/tsc/product/little-farmer-products-homegrown-bugs-non-gmo-black-soldier-fly-grubs-and-grains-5-lb-lfp156) (marketing claim)

### Inferences
- **Label guarantees.** For a full-fat whole dried larva at about 5% moisture, realistic as-fed guarantees would be crude protein ≥35% and crude fat ≥25–30%. These must be confirmed by laboratory proximate analysis for each substrate mix, because fat can swing by 15 or more percentage points with substrate.
- **Tryento's substrates:**
  - Brewer's spent grain, wheat bran and bagasse are fibrous and low in oil, so they will probably give lower fat than oil-rich food waste.
  - Dairy or yogurt waste may raise fat and energy.
  - A high calcium content makes the product attractive as a laying-hen and reptile supplement. Reptile keepers value a favourable Ca:P ratio.
- **24 h fasting.** Fasting before processing reduces gut contents. It probably lowers ash and substrate residue and improves hygiene, but no quantitative source was retrieved in this session.

### Gaps
- A side-by-side comparison of larva versus prepupa composition (protein, fat, chitin, Ca) was not captured in this session. Feedipedia and Spranghers focus on prepupae.
- Chitin content as % DM was not captured (typically cited around 5–10% DM, but not verified here).
- No composition data was found for BSFL reared specifically on brewer's spent grain, bagasse or dairy waste.

## 7. Feeding-trial evidence: inclusion rates and results

### Takeaway
Across species, moderate inclusion is safe and often neutral-to-positive:
- about 5–10% of the diet for laying hens and broilers
- 5–10% for weaned piglets
- about 25–50% fishmeal replacement for trout and tilapia
- about 10–25% for shrimp

Higher levels (for example 18% full-fat BSFL in layers, or ≥15% in some broiler trials) tend to depress intake, egg output or feed conversion (FCR). The chitin and fat loads are the usual explanations.

### Cited Findings
- **Laying hens, whole larvae (5% of DM intake).** Whole dehydrated BSFL supplement for 15 weeks (hens aged 23–38 weeks) — [PMC11394493](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11394493/):
  - no differences in egg production, egg weight, egg mass or FCR
  - higher Haugh units in the first sub-period
  - lower plasma cholesterol and triglycerides at 32 weeks
  - no effect on yolk colour or shell
- **Free-range laying hens, 17 weeks, 0%, 10% or 18% full-fat dried BSFL** — [Agriculture 2024, 14:31](https://doi.org/10.3390/agriculture14010031) (search snippet; full text blocked):
  - Controls gained more weight and laid more and bigger eggs on less feed, with better FCR, than hens on 18%.
  - The 10% hens' live weight did not differ from the 18% hens' until the last weighing.
- **Feedipedia summaries across trials** — [Feedipedia](https://www.feedipedia.org/node/16388):
  - Layers: 3%, 6% or 9% defatted BSFL meal maintained production, with declines at the highest levels.
  - Broilers: 5%, 10% and 15% tested; 15% impaired FCR in one study, while up to 20% increased weight gain in another.
  - Weaned pigs: 5% or 10% partially defatted meal had no adverse effect; 4% or 8% full-fat larvae maintained performance under challenge.
  - Growing/finishing pigs: 50–100% fishmeal replacement improved performance.
  - Salmonids: 25% or 50% fishmeal replacement.
  - Tilapia: 10–100% fishmeal replacement.
  - Catfish: best at 25% replacement.
- **Broiler meta-analysis** (15 studies, 2015–2025) — [PMC13382029](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13382029/) (search snippet):
  - no significant overall effect on feed intake (ADFI), daily gain (ADG) or FCR
  - higher inclusion was associated with lower ADFI
  - meat had more muscle fat, more redness and higher ultimate pH
- **Second broiler meta-analysis:** no performance effect at BSFL meal levels of 5.7 ± 2.3% and 15.5 ± 6.0% of the diet — [Ital. J. Anim. Sci. 2023 meta-analysis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1828051X.2023.2194909) (search snippet)
- **Piglets:** partially defatted BSF meal at 0%, 5% or 10% in isonitrogenous, isoenergetic diets — [Biasato et al. 2019, PMC6379995](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6379995/) (search snippet)
- **Fish and shrimp**, as summarised in [Frontiers in Aquaculture 2026 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aquaculture/articles/10.3389/faquc.2026.1862744/full):
  - Tilapia: up to 100% fishmeal replacement without adverse effects on growth, feed use, survival or blood values (Tippayadara 2021); best growth at 25–50% (Muin 2017).
  - Rainbow trout: up to 50% replacement kept weight gain and FCR (St-Hilaire 2007; Stamer 2014); 26.4% dietary inclusion lowered specific growth (Dumas 2018).
  - Atlantic salmon: total replacement possible (Belghit 2019); another study found 12.5% replacement acceptable, with reduced digestibility above that (Weththasinghe 2021).
  - Pacific white shrimp: 10–20% replacement kept growth, which declined at 30% (Chen 2021); about 25% maximum (Cummins 2017).
- **Cats:** 20% dried whole BSFL in diets for pregnant and lactating queens and for kittens gave similar intake, body weight and litter size. Kittens ate more, weighed more at 6–10 weeks after weaning and had higher protein digestibility — [PMC12023938](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12023938/)

### Inferences
- **Guidance for Tryento's customers:**
  - Backyard layers: treat-level feeding of whole dried larvae (about 5% of intake, roughly 5–6 g per hen per day for a hen eating about 110 g/day) matches the neutral-to-positive trial evidence. The 110 g/day intake is my assumption.
  - Warn against replacing more than about 10% of the ration with full-fat larvae.
  - Tilapia and other fish: partial fishmeal replacement at 25–50% is well supported.
- **Whole versus meal.** Most trials use defatted meal. Evidence on whole full-fat larvae (the Tryento product) is thinner but consistent at 5–10%.

### Gaps
- Full-text inclusion data for live-larvae poultry trials (welfare and behaviour benefits) were not extracted.
- Trials in Latin America (Venezuela, Colombia, Brazil) with local substrates were not captured.
- Laying-hen trials with ≤10% whole larvae in tropical conditions were not captured.

## 8. Regulation and safety: EU, US, Latin America (Venezuela, Brazil, Colombia, Mexico, Peru, Argentina) and contaminants

### Takeaway
The EU and Brazil have the most detailed insect-feed rules. Both require:
- heat-processed insects only (no raw insects for food-producing animals)
- a ruminant feed ban
- plant-based or approved substrates, with manure, catering waste and table scraps prohibited
- registered processing sites

The US allows dried BSFL raised only on feed-grade feedstock, for fish, poultry, swine and adult dogs and cats, at ≤10% moisture.

I found **no Venezuelan regulation specific to insects as feed.** In Venezuela, insect products would fall under INSAI's general registration of animal-feed products and raw materials. Colombia (ICA) and Mexico (SENASICA) also have no insect-specific rules that I could find.

Cadmium is the key chemical hazard for BSF, with bioaccumulation factors above 6.

### Cited Findings

**European Union**
- Reg. (EU) 2017/893 lifted the TSE feed ban for insect processed animal protein (PAP) in **aquaculture** feed for 7 species, including *H. illucens*. Before 2021, insect PAP could also be used in pet food, feed for fur animals and feed for other non-food-producing animals such as reptiles, birds of prey and zoo animals — [IPIFF FAQ, April 2021](https://ipiff.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Apr-13-2021-Frequently-asked-questions-insect-PAPs.pdf); [Reg. 2017/893, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/893/oj/eng)
- Commission Reg. (EU) **2021/1372** (17 August 2021) authorised insect PAP in **poultry and pig** feed under the same conditions as for aquaculture. It entered into force on 7 September 2021. Conditions — [IPIFF FAQ](https://ipiff.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Apr-13-2021-Frequently-asked-questions-insect-PAPs.pdf); [IPIFF legislation page](https://ipiff.org/insects-eu-legislation-general/); [EUR-Lex 2021/1372](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2021/1372/oj/eng):
  - PAP must be made in plants approved under Art. 24(1)(a) of Reg. 1069/2009 that are "dedicated exclusively to the production of products derived from farmed insects".
  - Compound feed containing insect PAP must be made in establishments dedicated to aquaculture, poultry or pig feed, with possible derogations for physically separate lines.
- **Whole insects (EU):**
  - IPIFF (2021): "killed whole insects (with or without any further treatment) — but not 'processed' according to Regulation (EC) No 1069/2009 — may not be used as feed for food-producing animals" — [IPIFF FAQ](https://ipiff.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Apr-13-2021-Frequently-asked-questions-insect-PAPs.pdf)
  - IPIFF also says the specific PAP processing methods "[are] not prescribed, in case the operator produces insect fat, whole insects or if the insect meal is obtained through hydrolysis" — [IPIFF legislation page](https://ipiff.org/insects-eu-legislation-general/)
  - My reading: EU whole dried larvae for hens or pigs must be heat-processed under the animal by-products rules in an approved plant. The exact method is less prescriptive than for meal. **I did not verify the status of *live* insects fed to poultry or pigs, or later amendments, in this session.**
- **Reg. (EU) 2021/1925** (November 2021):
  - added silkworm (*Bombyx mori*), making 8 authorised species — [IPIFF legislation page](https://ipiff.org/insects-eu-legislation-general/)
  - set the rules for **frass**: ≥70 °C for ≥60 min; *E. coli* or Enterococcaceae <3 log CFU/g after treatment; *Salmonella* absent in storage; reduction of spore-formers where they are a relevant hazard — [PMC12120769](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12120769/) (search snippet)
- **EU substrates** — [IPIFF legislation page](https://ipiff.org/insects-eu-legislation-general/):
  - Allowed: materials of vegetable origin, plus limited animal-origin materials: milk, eggs and their products; honey; rendered fat; blood products from non-ruminants.
  - Prohibited: slaughterhouse and rendering products, **manure**, **catering waste**, and unsold supermarket or food-industry products containing meat or fish.
- **Ruminant ban.** Insect PAP cannot be fed to ruminants — [IPIFF FAQ](https://ipiff.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Apr-13-2021-Frequently-asked-questions-insect-PAPs.pdf)
- **Microbiological criteria for insect PAP:** *Salmonella* absent in 25 g; Enterobacteriaceae n = 5, c = 2, m = 10, M = 300 CFU/g — [Reg. 142/2011 Annex X](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2011/142/annex/X)
- **EFSA 2015 risk profile:**
  - Prion hazard in non-processed insects is expected to be "equal or lower" than in currently authorised animal proteins, "as long as the insects are fed on substrates that do not harbour material of ruminant or human (manure) origin".
  - Hazards depend on production method, substrate, harvest stage, species, life stage and processing.
  - Data on the transfer of contaminants from substrate to insect are "very limited".

  — [EFSA Journal 2015;13(10):4257](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2015.4257)
- **Heavy-metal limits.** Maximum limits for As, Pb, Cd and Hg in feed are set by Directive 2002/32/EC and are expressed relative to feed at 12% moisture. Hg is 0.1 mg/kg in animal-origin feed materials and 0.2 mg/kg in complete feed. Cd is 1 mg/kg in vegetable-origin feed materials — [Trends Food Sci Technol 2023, citing 2002/32/EC](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924224423003199) (search snippet)

**United States**
- **AAFCO 60.117 "Dried Black Soldier Fly Larvae"** was moved to Official at the January 2025 midyear meeting (minutes finalised 7 March 2025). The definition — [AAFCO IDC minutes Jan 2025](https://www.aafco.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IDC_Minutes_MY_2025.pdf):
  - "the dried larvae of the Black Soldier Fly, *Hermetia illucens*, with or without mechanical extraction of part of the oil, that has been raised on feedstock composed exclusively of feed grade materials"
  - label with minimum crude protein and minimum crude fat as-fed, plus maximum crude fat if defatted
  - "dried by artificial means to no more than 10% moisture"
  - "for use in finfish, poultry, and swine feed, and in adult dog food, and adult cat food"
- AAFCO's SUIP No. 9 amendment to cover insects for wild birds and exotic species was still in work-group stage as of January 2025 — [same source](https://www.aafco.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IDC_Minutes_MY_2025.pdf)

**Brazil (MAPA)** — the most detailed Latin American framework found
- MAPA authorised BSF larva meal for animal feed in November 2020 through Instrução Normativa (IN) 110/2020 — [Jornal da USP / search results](https://jornal.usp.br/ciencias/insetos-no-cardapio-cientistas-aprimoram-a-producao-de-alimentos-feitos-com-larvas-de-mosca/) (search snippet; the IN text was not read)
- **Ofício-Circular 33/2023/CGI/DIPOA/SDA/MAPA (7 July 2023)** — [MAPA circular (full text)](https://wikisda.agricultura.gov.br/manual_fiscalizacao_aa/manual_autocontrole_aa/circ_2023-33_aa_processamento_de_insetos.pdf):
  - **Registration:**
    - Insect processors (larvae included) must register in the animal-feed area as FABRICANTE, flagged "PROCESSADOR DE INSETOS".
    - Rearing-only farms and substrate suppliers are *not* subject to feed-area registration, but must be regular with the competent body.
    - Insect products classed as ingredients (animal origin) must be registered; products classed as "alimentos" are exempt (IN 51/2020).
    - Only species on MAPA's list of authorised ingredients may be used. New species follow IN 3/2021.
  - **Target animals:** animal protein and fat, including from insects, are banned for ruminants (IN 8/2004). Labels must say "Uso Proibido na Alimentação de Ruminantes" (IN 22/2009 for ingredients; IN 30/2009 for feeds).
  - **Heat treatment:** "raw insects, not heat-treated, are not admitted in the feeding of production animals".
  - **Allowed substrates:**
    - blood products; hydrolysed proteins (including from ruminant hides); gelatine and collagen
    - eggs; milk and dairy products and colostrum; honey; rendered fats
    - non-ruminant organic residues such as pre-consumer expired or returned food from retail, Ceasa and supermarkets
    - plant by-products of the food and feed industries
    - feed-grade products from MAPA-registered firms, including animal meals
    - dry dog and cat food, even when expired
  - **Prohibited substrates:** meal and table scraps, leachate (*chorume*), faeces, and raw animal residues. **Reusing substrate is prohibited.**
  - **Good practices:** SOPs covering the substrate, receiving checks, separation, cooking (temperature, time and monitoring), dehydration (parameters and proof of uniformity), storage, and microbiological specifications based on literature or international legislation.

**Contaminants (heavy metals)**
- Van der Fels-Klerx et al. 2016 — [PLOS ONE 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0166186):
  - **Cadmium** accumulated in BSF larvae in every treatment, with a bioaccumulation factor above 6. Larval Cd exceeded the EU limits even at lower exposures.
  - **Lead** had a bioaccumulation factor of about 1.2–1.4.
  - **Arsenic** had a bioaccumulation factor of 0.5–0.6 and was mostly excreted.
  - The authors say EU maximum limits "should be revised per insect species".
- A 2023 review — [Environ Monit Assess 2023 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10661-023-11186-w) (search snippet):
  - Cd mainly stays in the larval body, with retention up to about 93%.
  - Pb, Hg and As are mostly excreted via exuviae and faeces.
  - Cd, As and Pb in BSFL "often exceed" feed and food standards.
- Heavy-metal bioaccumulation "was more likely to occur in brewery waste than in other substrates" — [Ital. J. Anim. Sci. 2024, minerals and heavy metals across substrates](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1828051X.2024.2302845) (search snippet; attribution not full-text verified)

### Inferences
- **Venezuelan regulatory route.** Absent insect-specific rules, the likely route is to register the dried larvae with INSAI as a "materia prima" or feed product (ABA) for animal use, with a label, technical sheet, composition or QC certificate and possibly an environmental plan. Use SIGMAV movement guides for transport. Confirm directly with INSAI; the status is uncertain.
- **Voluntary compliance.** Aligning voluntarily with EU and Brazil rules is the most defensible approach and would ease any future export or registration. That means:
  - no manure, catering or table-scrap substrates
  - no substrate reuse
  - heat treatment before drying
  - a "not for ruminants" label
  - *Salmonella* and Enterobacteriaceae testing
- **Tryento's substrates are all allowed** under both the EU and Brazilian rules: brewer's spent grain, bagasse and wheat bran (plant by-products) and yogurt/dairy waste (milk products).
- **Cadmium is the main substrate-linked risk,** especially with brewer's spent grain. Test Cd (and Pb) in the larvae at least once per substrate source, and again after any supplier change.
- **Label contents** to meet most regimes:
  - species
  - "whole dried larvae"
  - guaranteed minimum crude protein, minimum crude fat, maximum fibre and maximum moisture (as-fed)
  - target species
  - "Prohibido su uso en la alimentación de rumiantes"
  - lote and best-before date
  - storage instructions
