# BSF farms as businesses and facilities: industry, operations, automation and economics (status as of Sept 2026)

Conventions used below: **[CO]** = company-sourced or company-sponsored figure (commercial bias; not independently verified). **[MR]** = commercial market-research firm (methodology opaque). **[snippet]** = figure seen only in a search-engine summary of the linked page, not read in full; treat as lower confidence. Dates are publication dates of the source unless stated.

## 1. Industry landscape 2025–2026: major producers, scale, funding, closures, pivots, market size

### Takeaway
The Western "mega-factory" model has largely failed or retreated: Ÿnsect was liquidated (Dec 2025), ENORM went bankrupt (late 2025), Inseco closed (2025), Agronutris is under court protection, AgriProtein collapsed in 2021, InnovaFeed paused its US plant (Aug 2025), and Protix/Tyson shelved Nebraska while Protix moves to Southeast Asia (2026). The two surviving European BSF leaders operate at roughly 15–50 kt/yr of larvae. They are joined by lean, modular, semi-automated producers in low-cost tropical geographies. Real physical supply is small: more than 15,000 t of insect meal worldwide in 2026 [CO]. Market-size forecasts disagree with each other by roughly 10×.

- AgriProtein (South Africa/UK, BSF) opened its first full-scale Cape Town plant in 2014 and raised $105M in 2018. It planned 100 factories by 2024 and 200 by 2027. It lost $38.9M in 2019, entered administration in Feb 2021, and its assets went to Prezero U.S. for $10M in June 2021. Stated causes: over-ambitious expansion, a labour-intensive plant hit by COVID distancing rules, restaurant-closure disruption to its feedstock supply, large headcount with underused capacity, no product–market fit, and feedstocks that were not properly characterised ("not all wastes are the same"). — [Lux Research, 5 Jul 2023](https://luxresearchinc.com/blog/what-happened-to-agriprotein/)

- Hexafly (Ireland, BSF) came out of a SCARP rescue (court-supervised restructuring) in Nov 2023. Its current status could not be confirmed. — [CB Insights profile](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/hexafly) [snippet]

### Inferences
- The two leaders report more than 15 kt of meal globally. At roughly $2–3.5/kg (see Economics), that is a meal market of only tens of millions of dollars. The $1bn+ "BSF market" figures must therefore include live larvae, pet treats, frass, oil, equipment and services, or be inflated. The report writer should not treat these forecasts as physical demand.
- Every high-profile failure shares the same pattern: large capex bets before unit economics were proven, EU/North American energy and labour costs, and restricted or costly feedstock. The survivors' pattern is phased and modular builds, semi-automation, cheap or gate-fee feedstock, low-cost tropical geography, and premium niches (pet food, aquafeed functional benefits, frass). This profile favours a small tropical Venezuelan farm, provided it does not copy European capex-heavy automation.
- Protix's 2026 move to Southeast Asia suggests that tropical, low-energy-cost operation is now seen as the structurally advantaged position. That is directly relevant to Venezuela's lowland climate.

### Gaps
- Current (2025–26) status and tonnage for Enterra (Canada), Grubbly Farms (US), Nasekomo (Bulgaria), BioflyTech (Spain), Hexafly (Ireland) and Entocycle's own production were not verified; searches only returned market-report listings.
- Protix's current capex, cost per kg and profitability are not public. InnovaFeed's cost claims are relative ("÷7") with no absolute €/kg.
- The Fish Site (Oct 2025) and AgFunderNews (Nov 2025) disagree on the month of ENORM's bankruptcy.

## 2. Latin America: companies, farms, universities, programmes (including Venezuela)

### Takeaway
Latin American BSF activity is real but mostly small: startups, university spin-offs, municipal pilots and backyard/smallholder farms. Costa Rica (ProNuvo) is the clearest example of an investor-backed commercial scale-up. Brazil has at least one announced large waste-processing project (EntoFood) plus an industry association. The only Venezuelan activity found is university extension work (UCV/MIZA, Maracay), publicised by the national poultry federation.

## 3. Farm models and scale (backyard, smallholder, containerised/modular, industrial); tropical operation without full climate control

### Takeaway
There is a continuum. At one end are unheated backyard bins. Next come Eawag-style semi-open 1–5 t waste/day facilities in the tropics, run by 1–3 rearing workers with crates, pallets, shade and fans. Then come modular or "capital-light" plants of a few thousand t/yr in SE Asia. At the top are fully climate-controlled vertical factories (Protix, InnovaFeed) processing tens to hundreds of kt of feedstock. In the tropics, the viable small-to-mid model relies on ambient climate, shading, airflow and natural light for mating, not HVAC.

### Cited Findings
- **Eawag/Dortmans model (Indonesia-derived)**: the manual is based on "a one ton of waste per day treatment facility in Indonesia that has been in operation for over two years" (fruit/vegetable market waste) and a Swedish experimental site. The authors say its procedures are "valid for a large range of scaling-up". — [Dortmans et al., Eawag 2017, p.1](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf). The 2nd edition (Dortmans, Egger, Diener, Zurbrügg 2021) covers a facility able to treat 5 t/day — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353923113_Black_Soldier_Fly_Biowaste_Processing_-_A_Step-by-Step_Guide_2nd_Edition)
- The Eawag model targets "low-income settings that rely mostly on simple technology and unskilled labour". Climate management is mainly passive: a "Closed and ventilated room for the rearing, but sunlight for the love cages"; a "Sheltered area without direct sunlight for the treatment containers"; stacked crates with open "ventilation frames" between levels; and fans over the stacks "during the last few days" to dry the residue. — [Eawag 2017](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Eawag rearing-unit capacity vs work schedule** (Table 2; column alignment reconstructed from PDF extraction):
  - Every day, full capacity: 21.0 t waste/week, about 14 million 5-day-old larvae (5-DOL)/week, 3 workers, about 40 m², stable output.
  - Every day, half capacity: 10.5 t/week, about 7 million/week, 2 workers, about 35 m².
  - Mon–Wed–Fri: 9.0 t/week, about 6 million/week, 1 worker, with larger fluctuation in daily output.
  - Source: [Eawag 2017, ch.4](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **ICIPE / East Africa**: ICIPE, funded by the IKEA Foundation, is scaling BSF farming combined with "push-pull" vegetable cropping in Kenya, Rwanda and Uganda — [IKEA Foundation](https://ikeafoundation.org/grants/scaling-regenerative-black-soldier-fly-farming-along-with-vegetable-push-pull-cropping-systems-in-rural-kenya-rwanda-and-uganda/). The model uses 2 Centralised Processing and Egg Production (CPEP) hubs, in Kenya and Rwanda, that hold the mother colony and supply smallholders, which amounts to a hub-and-spoke egg/neonate supply. Kenya's NAVCDP surveyed 373 smallholder BSF farmers across 12 counties in June–July 2024. — [ICIPE project document](https://www.icipe.org/sites/default/files/phd-projects-vippt-2023.pdf) [snippet]
- Kenyan smallholder anecdote (media, unverified): a farmer started with KSh 15,000 and earns more than KSh 50,000/month — [The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/sports/money/article/2001460656/i-started-black-soldier-fly-business-with-only-sh15k) [snippet; anecdotal]. An academic study of profitability determinants for Kenyan BSF enterprises exists — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356567273_Determinants_of_profitability_of_black_soldier_fly_farming_enterprise_in_Kenya) (not read)
- **Modular/containerised**: Protenga runs a "modular, capital-light 'smart insect farm'". Tebrio uses a "modular and phased development model". FreezeM sells frozen/paused neonates as "breeding-as-a-service" to 40 customers and observes a shift toward smaller, modular facilities. Chapul moved from one big plant to phased construction. — [AgFunderNews, Mar 2026](https://agfundernews.com/insect-ag-recalibrates-after-a-brutal-shakeout-where-are-the-key-players-now). A UK micro-unit for on-farm feed costs about £100,000 per unit (≈75% of the average UK farm's annual fixed costs) — [Frontier Economics, Nov 2023](https://www.frontier-economics.com/media/o12jxgjt/frontier-economics_delivering-insect-feed-at-scale_final-report_01-12-23.pdf)
- **Industrial vertical**: Protix's Nebraska design was 250,000 t/yr of waste producing 70,000 t/yr of live-larvae equivalent — [GlobalPETS, Apr 2026](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/protix-and-tyson-foods-halt-investment-in-us-insect-facility/). Frontier's UK cost model uses three site sizes: large (250,000 t waste/yr, £50M start-up, 40 FTE, about 12,500 t protein/yr), medium (70,000 t, £16M, 25 FTE, about 3,500 t) and small (20,000 t, £7.5M, 10 FTE, about 1,000 t). These figures come from operator interviews. — [Frontier Economics, Nov 2023, Annex B](https://www.frontier-economics.com/media/o12jxgjt/frontier-economics_delivering-insect-feed-at-scale_final-report_01-12-23.pdf)
- Western startups "were forced into high automation, expensive climate control, and high capex", while producers in India, SE Asia, Africa and LATAM can run lower-cost semi-automated systems — [search summary citing Meticulous Research](https://www.meticulousresearch.com/blog/378/why-insect-farming-startups-failed) [MR, snippet]
- Semi-controlled systems with shade nets and misting reportedly improved year-round consistency in Kenya trials; shade nets and passive ventilation reduce overheating in the tropics — [search summary of tropical rearing literature](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352186422001985) [snippet; primary trial not identified]
- Eawag's SIMBA ("Simplified BSF Approach") grow-out unit design exists as a low-tech reference — [Eawag SIMBA](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/SWIFT/SIMBA_Growout.pdf) (not read)

### Inferences
- Tryento sits at the Eawag scale (≤1–5 t waste/day). Eawag shows such a site can run on ambient tropical climate with passive measures and 1–3 rearing staff. Tryento's actively controlled mating cages (fans, atomiser, heater, LEDs) are closer to what industrial farms do for the breeding unit only. That is a sensible split: control the small, high-value breeding unit tightly; manage the bulk grow-out passively with shade, airflow and density.
- The hub-and-spoke pattern (ICIPE CPEP; FreezeM neonate supply) is a scaling option for Tryento: become the Venezuelan egg/neonate hub and supply satellite grow-out farms, rather than building all grow-out capacity itself.

### Gaps
- No detailed costed containerised BSF units (e.g., shipping-container turnkey systems) were found.
- No quantitative data from ICIPE on smallholder yields, climate, or seasonal variation in Kenya's warm lowlands (the closest analogue to lowland Venezuela).

## 4. Facility design: zoning, biosecurity, material flow, racks/trays, space per tonne

### Takeaway
Eawag's reference layout separates waste receiving and pre-processing, a treatment (grow-out) unit, a rearing unit (dark cages, love cages, nursery), harvesting, a lab, storage and a buffer zone. Rules of thumb are about 50 m² of nursery plus about 100 m² per tonne of incoming waste per day, and 40,000 five-day-old larvae per m² of treatment area fed 60 kg of waste over 12 days. Crates are stacked on pallets with ventilation frames between levels.

### Cited Findings
- **Siting**: steady cheap fresh waste; year-round access roads; avoid dense neighbourhoods; water, electricity and wastewater options; environmental buffers (trees, fences); and "downwind from the residential areas" — [Eawag 2017, §2](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Space rule**: "one can calculate with 50m2 for the nursery and 100m2 per ton of incoming waste per day". A sample layout for 2 t/day has zones of about: receiving/pre-processing 36 m², treatment unit 180 m², rearing (love cages, washing, drying) 50 m², nursery (dark cages plus nursery) 36 m², harvesting 36 m², lab 18 m², storage 18 m², buffer about 25 m². — [Eawag 2017, Fig. 5](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Treatment unit parameters**:
  - 10,000 5-DOL per "larvero" crate (40×60×17 cm) on 15 kg wet waste (75% water) for 12 days.
  - 40,000 5-DOL per m² fed 60 kg waste over 12 days, in three equal feedings on days 1, 5 and 8.
  - Waste depth over 5 cm leaves unprocessed bottom waste. Overfeeding creates a heating, fly-attracting layer; underfeeding slows growth.
  - Pallet stacks hold 36 larveros with 6 ventilation frames.
  - Source: [Eawag 2017, pp.16–17, T1](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Rearing unit hardware**:
  - Love cage: sturdy mosquito netting holding 6,000–10,000 flies, used for 6 days, then washed at 30 °C.
  - Dark cage: double-layer fabric with 16 pupation containers (60×40×12 cm), dismantled after about 2.5 weeks. Emergence starts about 10 days after setup and ends about day 25.
  - 10 wooden "eggies" per love cage (egg-laying media: 5 sheets of 25×5×0.3 cm).
  - Nursery containers 55×35×16 cm sit inside transfer boxes of 60×40×12 cm so prepupae self-harvest.
  - Ant traps (water cups) under every table leg. Frames and tables are sprayed with 95% alcohol.
  - Source: [Eawag 2017, steps R1–R8](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Material flow**: eggies → hatchling container (5 days on chicken feed) → 5-DOL either to treatment crates, or to nursery containers (about 2.5 weeks) for colony replacement → prepupae → pupation containers → dark cage → light-lured transfer to love cage → eggies. About 500 prepupae per pupation container per day for 5 days. — [Eawag 2017, R1–R8 and daily schedule](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Biosecurity/hygiene frameworks**: the IPIFF Guide on Good Hygiene Practices, updated Nov 2022 (9th version), covers feeding, breeding, killing, processing, storage and transport. It records microbiological criteria (Listeria monocytogenes, Salmonella, Enterobacteriaceae). HACCP is expected of food/feed operators, but for insect primary production its use "remains voluntary" — [IPIFF GHP page](https://ipiff.org/good-hygiene-practices/); [IPIFF GHP guide PDF](https://ipiff.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/IPIFF-Guide-on-Good-Hygiene-Practices.pdf) [snippet]. EU SUSINCHAIN also published good-practice guidelines for safe insect production (2024) — [SUSINCHAIN D6.6](https://susinchain.eu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/D6.6-Guidelines-for-good-practices-for-safe-insect-production.pdf) (not read)
- Aspire's cricket plant failed partly on equipment design (hoppers, conveyors, totes) and on copying a design from Texas into Ontario's climate. This is a cautionary example of transplanting a facility design between climates. — [AgFunderNews, Mar 2026](https://agfundernews.com/insect-ag-recalibrates-after-a-brutal-shakeout-where-are-the-key-players-now)

### Inferences
- By the Eawag rule, a 1 t/day Tryento site would need roughly 150–200 m² of built area (treatment about 90–100 m², rearing plus nursery about 50 m², plus lab and storage). Treatment area scales roughly linearly: at 5 kg waste/m²/day (60 kg/m² over 12 days), 1 t/day needs about 200 m² of crate footprint. Stacking on pallets divides the floor area by the number of levels.
- The ant-trap, washing and alcohol steps in Eawag's SOPs are cheap biosecurity controls worth adopting and logging in the PWA as inspection checklist items.
- A dedicated "hygiene zone" split (clean breeding unit vs dirty waste receiving and frass) is standard. Tryento's PWA already has separations and inspections; zone and room IDs on every record would support later HACCP-style traceability.

### Gaps
- No published m²-per-tonne figures for industrial vertical farms (Protix/InnovaFeed layouts are proprietary).
- No tropical-specific biosecurity data (e.g., wild BSF or housefly incursion, ant or predator pressure, mites) was found.

## 5. Climate control in tropical climates: ventilation vs cooling, larval heat, humidity, energy, CO2/NH3

### Takeaway
In BSF grow-out the main thermal problem is **heat produced by the larvae and microbes inside the substrate**, not cold. Substrate can reach 45–50 °C even when room air is below 30 °C. Industrial experience and research therefore point to ventilation and substrate-temperature monitoring as the critical controls, with heating mostly a temperate-climate issue. CO2 is the dominant gas emitted. Ammonia depends on substrate and harvest timing.

### Cited Findings
- **Larval heat**: in lab trials, doubling larval density raised substrate temperature by 0.6–2.4 °C. Peak substrate temperatures were 29.4 °C, 31 °C and 32.3 °C at 1.25, 2.5 and 5 larvae/cm². Bulking agents pushed peaks to about 45 °C and CO2 output to "up to 60 g per hour". Earlier large-scale trials reported "extreme temperatures of up to 50 °C" at 8,000 larvae/crate (2.9 larvae/cm²). The authors say "predicting thermogenesis could help reduce operational costs... by aligning heating and cooling processes with substrate temperature peaks". They logged substrate temperature at 1-min intervals with DS18B20 probes. — [Klammsteiner et al., iScience 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12205609/)
- **Industrial-scale evidence** (Portugal, EntoGreen): ambient air was 21.8–29.2 °C and 42–57% RH, yet **substrate ranged 21–47 °C**, occasionally approaching the upper thermal limit (about 47 °C). The setup was 20,000 larvae per 60×40 cm box on 13.5 kg of restaurant waste over 14 days. — [Oliveira et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13116064/)
- A 2023 review is dedicated to BSF "larval heat generation and management" — [Li et al., Insect Science 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1744-7917.13198) (abstract only). Heat accumulation at scale differs from lab trials "due to larger substrate volumes and population sizes, requiring better adapted ventilation, heating, and cooling" — [search summary of Klammsteiner/related](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12205609/) [snippet]
- **Temperature envelope**: optimal larval growth is at about 27–35 °C. The life cycle completes within about 15–37 °C, and 38–40 °C causes mortality. Higher temperatures speed development but raise mortality. — [search summaries of ScienceDirect 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352186422001985) and [PMC7355568](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7355568/) [snippet]
- **Humidity/moisture**: Eawag relies on air exchange between stacked crates to remove "moisture-saturated air" and supply oxygen, plus fans in the final days to produce a crumbly, sievable residue. It notes that "the intensity of active ventilation depends on the air humidity and the moisture content of the starting material". — [Eawag 2017](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf). Active ventilation can help larvae convert substrates with 80–90% water content — [search summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0956053X24006482) [snippet]
- **CO2/NH3**: CO2 is the main gas from BSF rearing, coming from larval and microbial respiration. In one mass-balance study, ammonia emissions were minimal and "could have been avoided if larvae had been harvested before the CO2 peak" — [Bioconversion efficiencies, GHG and NH3 emissions – mass balance, J. Cleaner Production 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S095965262032535X) [snippet]. CO2 and NH3 production is life-stage- and substrate-specific, driven by the energy and nutrient composition of the feed — [Bioresource Technology 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960852425017791) (title/abstract only). A "novel chamber system" for industry-relevant bioconversion characterisation, including gas measurement, is described in [Waste Management 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0956053X24006482) (not read)
- **Energy**: AgFunder's industry synthesis says climate control is "highly energy-intensive". Protix cites lower energy costs as a reason for moving to SE Asia. — [AgFunderNews, Mar 2026](https://agfundernews.com/insect-ag-recalibrates-after-a-brutal-shakeout-where-are-the-key-players-now); [GlobalPETS, Apr 2026](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/protix-and-tyson-foods-halt-investment-in-us-insect-facility/). Inseco was hurt by grid power outages (loadshedding) — [AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/insect-ag-recalibrates-after-a-brutal-shakeout-where-are-the-key-players-now). InnovaFeed attributes a large carbon advantage to "industrial symbiosis" (use of a neighbouring plant's waste heat) [CO] — [Food Ingredients First, Sep 2026](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/insect-protein-scale-carbon-cost.html)

### Inferences
- For a Venezuelan lowland site (ambient often 25–33 °C, high RH), the grow-out risk is **overheating of the substrate core**, especially at high density or with bulking agents, and **humidity/condensation** in stacks. A cold spell is not the risk. Tryento's cage heater is the least-needed actuator in grow-out. Ventilation (fans over and between stacked crates), shade, and density or feeding-rate control are the levers.
- Evaporative cooling (misting or wet pads) is cheap in the tropics but works against the humidity cap and substrate drying. In mating cages set to 72% ±3 RH, the atomiser adds both cooling and humidity, so the 9-case rule will sometimes face a conflict between cooling and staying under the RH cap. That conflict should be an explicit, logged decision, not a hidden outcome of rule order.
- Grid reliability matters. Inseco's loadshedding experience parallels Venezuelan outage risk. A UPS for the Pi and router, logging of power loss, and an "outage in progress" alert are proportionate.
- CO2 is the best cheap proxy for larval metabolic activity and for "is ventilation adequate?". NH3 is substrate-dependent (worse with manure or high-protein waste) and is a worker-comfort and larval-health issue.

### Gaps
- No published CO2 or NH3 setpoints (ppm) or air-change rates for BSF rearing rooms were found. Vendor guidance (Manna Insect "climatization" page) returned 404.
- No numeric energy use (kWh per kg larvae) for ventilation or cooling in tropical BSF farms was found.
- No data on heat output per kg of larvae (W/kg) was found in accessible sources.

## 6. Sensing, IoT and automation: sensors, control strategies, sensor quality, alerting, platforms, open-source systems

### Takeaway
Industrial leaders automate **dosing and counting of neonates (machine vision), feeding, and climate in chambers**. Research systems add **substrate-temperature probes, thermal cameras, CO2 and gas logging**. Hobby/academic IoT builds (ESP32/Raspberry Pi with DHT22, MQTT, Firebase) resemble Tryento's stack but are simple bang-bang loops without alerting. The DHT22 is the weak link: poor accuracy (about ±5% RH in practice), temperature-dependent drift, and about a one-year life. Control loops should use hysteresis or deadband plus minimum on/off timers.

### Cited Findings
**What commercial and research farms sense and automate**
- Entocycle (UK) runs a farm under London Bridge railway arches with robotic arms, climate-controlled chambers, a Siemens digital twin used before construction, and machine vision counting "3,000 microscopic larvae a second with 96–100% accuracy". It claims survival improved by 30% [CO] — [The Manufacturer, 10 Jun 2025](https://www.themanufacturer.com/articles/siemens-partners-with-entocycle-to-digitalise-insect-farming/). Its Entosight Neo neonate doser processes up to 4,000 neonates/s [CO] — [Entocycle LinkedIn](https://zm.linkedin.com/posts/entocycle_black-soldier-fly-neonate-counter-activity-6943194163597205505-wJ_t) [snippet]. Trials at EnviroFlight's R&D centre gave 4.52% mean absolute percentage error in dosing target neonates, a "substantial improvement" per EnviroFlight's president [CO] — [Entocycle press release](https://entocycle.com/press-releases/trials-of-entocycles-optical-dosing-solution-at-enviroflight-a-success)
- Protix is described as using real-time environmental monitoring, automated feeding and robotics — [Discover Agriculture 2025 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44279-025-00194-8) [snippet; secondary]
- Research computer vision: YOLOv8 segmentation to predict larval sex and morphological traits (Nawoya et al.) — [Smart Agricultural Technology 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772375525001868). Deep learning with optical flow for automatic detection of larval stages — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2214317325000204). A review of computer vision and deep learning for insects-as-food/feed — [Nawoya et al. review (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Sarah-Nawoya-2/publication/376380302_Computer_vision_and_deep_learning_in_insects_for_food_and_feed_production_A_review/links/657755e6ea5f7f02055fa57a/Computer-vision-and-deep-learning-in-insects-for-food-and-feed-production-A-review.pdf)
- UC Riverside "smart bin"/"smart lid" (Urrutia Avila, Karydis et al., 2022): thermal imaging camera, classical computer-vision sensors and abiotic sensors, with automatic substrate aeration as a "soft real time system". It produced larvae "on par with manual techniques". — [arXiv 2207.04335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04335)
- Klammsteiner et al. logged substrate temperature every minute with DS18B20 probes and argue substrate-temperature logging is "essential" for reproducibility — [iScience 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12205609/)
- Small-farm IoT examples: an ESP32 with DHT22, a soil-moisture sensor, a DC exhaust fan via an L298N driver, and Firebase for a BSF "biopond". It is framed as replacing manual monitoring by small breeders — [JAIEA](https://ioinformatic.org/index.php/JAIEA/article/view/2558). MQTT data communication for a BSF larvae monitoring system — [ResearchGate 2022](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365224145_Data_Communication_using_MQTT_for_Black_Soldier_Fly_Larvae_Monitoring_System)

**Sensor quality (DHT22 vs alternatives)**
- An independent bench test of many low-cost hygrometers against salt references (Robert Smith) found:
  - DHT22/AM2302 had "typical errors around 5%" RH, and he "never got them to meet their advertised accuracy".
  - DHT22s showed "strong calibration drift as a function of temperature".
  - Of 6 DHT22s, "average life expectancy was around a year. Four are now dead."
  - BME280 met "±3% at the reference temperature of 25 °C" with "almost no drift as a function of varying temperature" and was his favourite.
  - Source: [kandrsmith.org](https://www.kandrsmith.org/RJS/Misc/Hygrometers/overview_conclusions.html)
- Vendor or maker blogs (lower rigour) claim:
  - SHT31: ±2% RH and ±0.2 °C, with less than 0.5% RH/yr drift. An integrated heater can drive off condensation, which DHT22 and BME280 cannot.
  - DHT22: can drift up to 5% over 12 months with VOCs or temperature cycling; clones show severe drift above 80% RH.
  - Sources: [Zbotic](https://zbotic.in/sht31-vs-dht22-vs-bme280-best-humidity-sensor-for-your-project/); [Wavtron](https://www.wavtron.in/blog/dht22-vs-bme280-vs-sht31)

**Control strategy**
- On/off control with little hysteresis "can cause chattering if there are small amounts of noise near the set point". Practical controllers use a deadband in which "the controller does nothing". — [Omron glossary](https://www.ia.omron.com/support/guide/53/explanation_of_terms.html); [InstrumentationTools](https://instrumentationtools.com/onoff-control-principle/)
- Insufficient hysteresis causes rapid cycling, contact wear and unstable temperature. Hysteresis should be chosen from thermal mass, sensor placement and load inertia; too much produces overshoot or late fan engagement — [ERSA Electronics](https://www.ersaelectronics.com/blog/temperature-regulator-relay-guide)
- "Minimum runtime and cooldown guard timers" prevent short cycling. This is a common embedded pattern, for example in an open-source aquaculture pump controller — [GitHub aqua-sense issue #22](https://github.com/AARVAK-VSET/aqua-sense/issues/22)

### Inferences
- **Tryento vs industry**: Tryento's Pi + DHT22 + relays + MongoDB/Redis buffer is at or above the level of published small-farm IoT builds (ESP32/Firebase). The industry gap is not the controller; it is **(a) alerting, (b) substrate-core temperature, (c) CO2, (d) automated counting and weighing**. These are exactly Tryento's listed gaps.
- **Cheapest high-value additions**, ranked:
  1. Waterproof DS18B20 probes in grow-out substrate (about 1-min logging, as in the published research) with over-temperature alerts at about 40 °C and above.
  2. Push alerts on: sensor disagreement between each cage's two DHT22s beyond a set tolerance; stale or no data; out-of-band readings for N consecutive cycles; relay commanded ON with no measurable effect.
  3. One NDIR CO2 sensor per room.
  4. Replacing DHT22s with SHT3x/SHT4x (heater helps at 72%+ RH near atomisers) or BME280.
  5. A load cell or scale workflow tied to the PWA for love-cage, eggie and crate weights.
- **Averaging two DHT22s hides failures**. With about a one-year life and ±5% typical error, the controller should treat each sensor independently: reject out-of-range or frozen values, fall back to the healthy sensor, and alert. It should also enter a safe default if both fail, e.g., exhaust fan on, heater off, atomiser duty-cycled, rather than acting on garbage.
- **Bang-bang every 20 s with ±1 °C / ±3% bands** will chatter near thresholds given DHT22 noise. Adding separate on/off thresholds (hysteresis), minimum on and off times per relay (e.g., tens of seconds to minutes, sized to cage thermal mass), and time-limited manual overrides that auto-expire matches standard practice. PID is unnecessary for on/off actuators; PWM or duty-cycling the atomiser is a simple improvement.
- Machine-vision neonate dosing is the flagship industrial automation, but it only pays at millions of neonates per day. At Tryento's scale, weighing (eggie mass, love-cage fly mass, crate mass) as in Eawag's SOPs gives most of the value.

### Gaps
- No published BSF-specific alerting practices or alarm thresholds from commercial farms (proprietary).
- No SHT4x long-term field data at high RH found (only SHT31 vendor specs).
- No fully open-source, complete BSF farm monitoring stack (hardware plus software) was found. Published systems are papers without repositories, or proprietary.
- "Digital twin" use in BSF appears limited to facility design (Entocycle/Siemens) rather than live process twins.

## 7. Operating KPIs, record-keeping, quality and traceability

### Takeaway
The core industrial and NGO KPI set is: waste reduction; bioconversion rate (dry larval mass ÷ dry substrate); FCR; larval yield; survival; eggs/neonates per day; flies per love cage; emergence rate; residue moisture; and drying yield (dried ÷ fresh, 25–35%). All are derived from disciplined **weighing** at every transfer and **oven-dry sampling** (105 °C, 24 h). Traceability frameworks (IPIFF GHP, HACCP) expect lot-level records from feed input to dispatch.

### Cited Findings
- **Eawag monitoring points**:
  - Love cages: weigh each love cage before and after connecting each dark cage to get fly mass and emergence rate; take a 20-fly reference sample to convert grams to fly count.
  - Eggies: weigh each eggie before insertion and after harvest; the net difference is egg mass. One egg weighs about 25 µg.
  - Grow-out: weigh each larvero's net content before emptying and the total larval harvest. Residue mass = content − larvae.
  - Sampling: take 50-larva and residue samples, dry at 105 °C for 24 h, and compute water content.
  - These give "waste reduction, bioconversion ratio and larval yield".
  - Source: [Eawag 2017, R1, R5, H1–H2](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- Eawag reference values: waste reduction 60–85% (dry weight) depending on waste, and up to 80% (wet weight). Larvae contain about 35% protein and about 30% fat. The dosing rule is 600–800 larvae per kg of wet waste. — [Eawag 2017](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- Eawag also uses date codes, colour-coded strings by day, labels with set-up date on every cage and container, and weekly handling schedules (a daily task matrix) — [Eawag 2017, ch.4](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Drying QC**: fresh larvae are about 70% water with water activity (aw) about 0.9; aw below 0.6 inhibits bacteria and yeasts. The dried/fresh output yield "lies typically between 25–35%"; a yield above 35% "implies that the drying process is not yet completed". — [Eawag drying factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
- **Benchmarks from literature** (substrate-dependent):
  - Bioconversion 22.96–30.71% in one set of treatments. FCR ranges from 1.78 (aquaculture sludge) to 4.11 (fish salad) and 3.82 (co-digested manure). Waste mass reduction of 50–80% in 10–14 days. — [search summary of PMC bioconversion studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12112482/); [aquaculture sludge study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10684894/) [snippet]
  - Industrial restaurant-waste trial (Portugal): BCR 18.34% (treated) vs 11.02% (control); FCR 5.48 vs 9.09 kg/kg; survival 69.3% vs 51.3%; growth 8.38 vs 6.59 mg/day over 14 days — [Oliveira et al. 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13116064/)
- Salomone et al.: 1 t of food waste yields about 30 kg of dried larvae plus 335 kg of matured compost — [Salomone et al., J. Cleaner Production 140 (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652616308411) [snippet]
- Frontier's cost model assumes an average bioconversion factor of 25%, a larvae-to-meal conversion factor of 45%, meal protein of 59%, and a 25% "refresh rate" (share of larvae retained for breeding). These are from interviews [CO-adjacent]. — [Frontier Economics 2023, Table 5](https://www.frontier-economics.com/media/o12jxgjt/frontier-economics_delivering-insect-feed-at-scale_final-report_01-12-23.pdf)
- **Traceability/HACCP**: insect food operators are expected to meet traceability, record-keeping and HACCP-based procedures. For insect producers, HACCP use is voluntary and on their own responsibility. IPIFF records microbiological limits. — [IPIFF GHP](https://ipiff.org/good-hygiene-practices/); [IPIFF regulatory brochure](https://ipiff.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/IPIFF-RegulatoryBrochure-update07-2020-1.pdf) [snippet]
- Batch-to-batch variation is listed among the sector's key barriers — [The Fish Site, Dec 2025](https://thefishsite.com/articles/insect-meal-can-the-sector-still-deliver-on-its-2030-promise)

### Inferences
- Tryento's PWA entities (colonies, egg harvests, trays, feedings, fasts, inspections, separations, oven runs, QC, packing, dispatch) already map onto the Eawag record set. The missing pieces are **mandatory weights at each transfer** plus **dry-matter samples**. With those, the Streamlit dashboard can compute:
  - bioconversion rate (dry larvae ÷ dry feed);
  - FCR (dry feed ÷ larval gain);
  - waste reduction;
  - survival (larvae in vs larvae out, via counts or weight ÷ unit weight);
  - eggs/day and g eggs per g flies;
  - drying yield (25–35% target) per oven run;
  - kg larvae per m² per day per tray position.
- A rough grow-out productivity benchmark combines two sources. Eawag loads 60 kg waste/m² per 12 days. Salomone's yield implies about 10% fresh larvae per wet waste (30 kg dry ≈ 100 kg fresh at 25–35% dry yield). Together that is about 6 kg fresh larvae/m²/cycle, or about 0.5 kg/m²/day of crate area. This is an inference, not a published KPI.
- A lot-ID chain (colony → egg lot → tray → harvest lot → oven run → pack → dispatch) is what IPIFF/HACCP traceability requires. Adding environmental data joined by room/cage and time turns the Pi logs into HACCP evidence (e.g., drying temperature/time as a critical control point).

### Gaps
- No public industrial values for labour hours per tonne, energy per kg (kWh/kg), eggs per day, or breeding-unit mortality from Protix/InnovaFeed (proprietary).
- No published "kg larvae per m² per day" industry benchmark found.
- The primary industrial benchmark set (e.g., Frontier's operator interviews) is not itemised per KPI.

## 8. Economics: capex/opex, cost per kg, prices, gate fees, break-even scale, why unit economics are hard

### Takeaway
BSF meal sells at roughly $2–3.5/kg in the EU/US (about $1.65–2.1/kg elsewhere) against soy at about $0.4–0.45/kg and fishmeal at about $1.7–1.8/kg. That is why feed-commodity plays fail. Costs are about half fixed (capex, utilities, labour), so utilisation and scale matter, but **feedstock cost or gate-fee revenue dominates**. Small tropical farms have reported costs from about $0.6–0.7/kg DM (unverified) to $1.84/kg meal (Benin). In Frontier's UK model, profitable scale against soy needs about £500/t meal, which needs roughly a £20/t gate fee plus large sites.

### Cited Findings
- **Prices**:
  - UK (Frontier, Nov 2023; commissioned by WWF/Tesco): BSF meal about £1,800/t; soy's "recent peak remained below £450 per tonne", with modelling at £351/t; fishmeal about £1,400/t. Replacing soy with BSF at current prices would raise meat retail prices about 50%; at £500/t the premium falls to 2–3%. Parity with soy on a protein basis is about £430/t of BSF meal. — [Frontier Economics](https://www.frontier-economics.com/media/o12jxgjt/frontier-economics_delivering-insect-feed-at-scale_final-report_01-12-23.pdf); sponsorship per [SalmonBusiness](http://www.salmonbusiness.com/tesco-wwf-sponsored-report-calculates-black-soldier-fly-meal-could-be-more-cost-effective-for-salmon-long-term/)
  - Average 2023 BSF protein-meal selling price was $2,160–3,250/t in the US and EU and $1,652–2,100/t in the rest of the world — [ScienceDirect 2025 review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666833525000553) [snippet]
  - Fishmeal averaged about $1,722/t in July 2025 — [aggregator, search summary](https://www.accio.com/plp/fish-meal-price-per-ton) [snippet; low-quality source]. Authoritative series: [IndexMundi](https://www.indexmundi.com/commodities/?commodity=fish-meal) and [FRED PFISHUSDA](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/PFISHUSDA) (not read)
- **Costs**:
  - Small-scale BSF meal in Benin: lowest estimated cost $1.84/kg — [Technical basis for small-scale BSF meal production in Benin, 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666154321000557) [snippet]
  - "20 commercial BSF farms in six countries (Brazil, Cambodia, China, Ghana, Kenya, Vietnam)" at $0.60–0.70/kg DM — reported only in a market-report press release — [Yahoo Finance/PR](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/global-black-soldier-fly-market-130500021.html) [MR; primary study not identified; treat as unverified]
  - Typical facility investment is $50,762–100,000 in most countries, versus $10,700–51,100 total facility expenses in Pakistan — [PMC 2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12386371/) [snippet]
- **Cost structure and scale (UK model)**:
  - "Approximately half of the cost base... [is] 'fixed'... capital expenditure, utilities and labour."
  - Processing 20% more waste at a medium site cuts price per tonne by only 7.5%.
  - Scaling from a small to a large site cuts about £400 per tonne of protein.
  - Start-up costs of £7.5M / £16M / £50M for 20k / 70k / 250k t waste/yr, with utilities of £200k / £700k / £2.5M per year and 10 / 25 / 40 FTE; 5-year payback assumed.
  - Source: [Frontier Economics 2023](https://www.frontier-economics.com/media/o12jxgjt/frontier-economics_delivering-insect-feed-at-scale_final-report_01-12-23.pdf)
- **Feedstock and gate fees (UK)**:
  - The permitted substrates are bakery surplus, dairy, vegetable by-products and brewers' grains, at a weighted cost of about £69/t. Spent brewers' grains cost about £40/t wet because farmers and fertiliser makers compete for them.
  - "To achieve a tolerable price of c.£500 per tonne, a large site would actually need to charge c.£20 per tonne of waste" as a gate fee.
  - Energy-from-waste gate fees are £20–150/t, which BSF could undercut.
  - Manure-based BSF protein would cost about £1,400/t; sewage sludge about £650/t (both currently not permitted in the EU/UK).
  - Source: [Frontier Economics 2023](https://www.frontier-economics.com/media/o12jxgjt/frontier-economics_delivering-insect-feed-at-scale_final-report_01-12-23.pdf)
- **Drying cost and energy**:
  - Eawag's small-scale methods, per kg of dried larvae: microwave "3,700 Watt" (unit as printed; likely Wh, i.e. about 3.7 kWh electricity), 0.9 kg/h throughput, 0.25 kg batches; sand-roasting in a pan 0.9 kg LPG; drum roaster 0.7 kg LPG at 6.2 kg/h; gas oven 0.7 kg LPG over 24 h at 65 °C for 30 kg batches.
  - Puffed (microwave or roasted) larvae have a bulk density of 110–130 g/L versus 220–230 g/L for oven-dried, and are "more appealing to customers and thus has a higher market value" (pet food).
  - Source: [Eawag drying factsheet](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/schwerpunkte/swm/Practical_knowhow_on_BSF/bsf_factsheet_drying.pdf)
  - A microwave-equipment vendor claims conventional dryers use 5–10 kWh per kg of dried larvae and that microwave drying halves operating costs [CO, vendor] — [Kerone](https://kerone.com/blog/microwave-drying-revolutionizes-black-soldier-fly-bsf-processing/). Microwave-dried BSF protein showed lower digestibility than oven-dried in research — [search summary](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1466856423002047) [snippet]
- **Why it is hard**: insects cost 2–10× soy or fishmeal (commentator) — [AgFunderNews](https://agfundernews.com/judicial-liquidation-for-ynsect-as-insect-farming-sector-struggles-to-become-competitive). Key barriers are energy costs, substrate restrictions, batch variation, capex, scaling complexity and waste-use regulation; insect inclusion "directly raises feed costs" — [The Fish Site, Dec 2025](https://thefishsite.com/articles/insect-meal-can-the-sector-still-deliver-on-its-2030-promise). Full Circle claims a 4–8% cost reduction versus comparable shrimp-feed formulations when combining BSFL with microbial protein [CO] — [AgFunderNews, Mar 2026](https://agfundernews.com/insect-ag-recalibrates-after-a-brutal-shakeout-where-are-the-key-players-now)
- **Premium niches**: InnovaFeed says clients are "prepared to pay a premium for nutritional performance and functional benefits" [CO] — [AgFunderNews, Jun 2026](https://agfundernews.com/innovafeed-raises-59m-scales-production-cuts-rd-headcount-as-insect-ag-hits-industrialization-phase). Entosystem sells mainly to the backyard-chicken market; Chapul and Keprea pivot to frass or fertiliser — [AgFunderNews, Mar 2026](https://agfundernews.com/insect-ag-recalibrates-after-a-brutal-shakeout-where-are-the-key-players-now)

### Inferences
- **Capex intensity (derived from Frontier)**: the small UK site produces about 1,000 t protein, or about 1,700 t meal at 59% protein, for £7.5M. That is roughly £4,400 per annual tonne of meal capacity, versus about £2,400 for the large site. Tryento will operate far below both, so its competitive lever is **low capex per tonne** (Eawag-style crates, pallets and shade) rather than scale economies.
- **Unit-economics levers for a Venezuelan small farm**, in rough order:
  1. Feedstock that is free or earns a fee: market, agro-industrial or brewery residues. There is no EU-style ban, but check local rules.
  2. Selling live or dried larvae into premium local niches (poultry and fish farmers, pet and bird food, aquaculture broodstock) rather than competing with fishmeal as commodity meal.
  3. Frass as a second revenue line.
  4. Cheap drying: microwave for small premium batches, gas or solar for bulk. Logging kWh or LPG per oven run is essential because drying is the largest energy cost the farm controls.
- A soy-competitive BSF meal price (about £430–500/t) is unreachable at small scale. Fishmeal-parity pricing (about $1.7/kg) is plausible only with gate fees or near-zero feedstock. A cost around $1.84/kg (the Benin small-scale estimate) is a realistic benchmark for an efficient tropical smallholder.

### Gaps
- No verified primary source for the "$0.60–0.70/kg DM across 20 farms" claim.
- No Latin American or Venezuelan cost-per-kg or price data for larvae, meal or frass was found.
- No itemised opex percentage split (feed %, labour %, energy %) from a peer-reviewed techno-economic study was retrieved (Niyonsaba et al. and Cadinu et al. were not accessed).
- Frass prices and gate-fee levels in Latin America are unknown.

## 9. Staffing, SOPs, training, worker safety (allergens, dust)

### Takeaway
At small scale BSF is labour-intensive but low-skill: Eawag runs a ≈1–3 t/day rearing unit with 1–3 workers, and industrial models assume 10–40 FTE for 20–250 kt/yr of waste. The underrated risk is **occupational allergy**: a small cohort of insect-farm employees showed high rates of sensitisation and bronchial hyperreactivity. PPE, dust control and health surveillance belong in SOPs.

### Cited Findings
- **Staffing**: Eawag's rearing unit needs 3 workers for 7-day full capacity (21 t waste/week), 2 for half capacity, and 1 on a Mon–Wed–Fri schedule (9 t/week) — [Eawag 2017, Table 2](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf). Frontier models 10 / 25 / 40 FTE for small / medium / large sites (20k / 70k / 250k t waste/yr) — [Frontier Economics 2023](https://www.frontier-economics.com/media/o12jxgjt/frontier-economics_delivering-insect-feed-at-scale_final-report_01-12-23.pdf). InnovaFeed has about 240 staff after cutting 60 of 300 [CO] — [AgFunderNews, Jun 2026](https://agfundernews.com/innovafeed-raises-59m-scales-production-cuts-rd-headcount-as-insect-ag-hits-industrialization-phase)
- **Labour intensity as a failure mode**: AgriProtein's labour-intensive operations suffered under COVID distancing rules, and its "large employee headcount" strained finances — [Lux Research](https://luxresearchinc.com/blog/what-happened-to-agriprotein/). Inseco cited "bad hiring decisions" — [AgFunderNews, Mar 2026](https://agfundernews.com/insect-ag-recalibrates-after-a-brutal-shakeout-where-are-the-key-players-now)
- **SOP structure**: Eawag's manual gives step-by-step tasks (R1–R8 rearing, W waste, T treatment, H harvest, P post-treatment). Each has an equipment list, "PROTECTIVE MEASURES FOR WORKERS" (lab coat and latex gloves for pupation containers, residue and 5-DOL; gloves, eye protection and protective clothing for boiling-water sanitising), and "POINTS OF MONITORING AND DATA COLLECTION". Weekly schedules use date codes. — [Eawag 2017](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)
- **Allergy (farm workers)**, from a small cohort study in J Allergy Clin Immunol Pract (2023):
  - 60% self-reported upper-respiratory symptoms from insect exposure.
  - 30.7% had a positive skin prick test to at least one insect; 58.3% a positive basophil activation test; 71.4% a positive histamine provocation test; 26.7% raised FeNO (above 25 ppb).
  - Half of the insect-sensitised had co-sensitisation to house dust mite.
  - Conclusion: "insect exposure leads to high levels of sensitization"; about two-thirds had bronchial hyperreactivity.
  - Source: [PubMed 37543086](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543086/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2213219823008140) [snippet; percentages imply about 12–15 subjects per test; species mix not confirmed as BSF]
- Occupational insect allergy is mostly respiratory (asthma, rhinoconjunctivitis) and sometimes cutaneous. It is documented among lab workers, aquaculture employees, farmers, bakers and sericulture workers — [Systematic review, IJMS 2023](https://ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9820383). BSF carries the pan-allergens tropomyosin and arginine kinase, which cross-react with crustaceans, mites and cockroaches — [PMC 2025 (BSF recombinant pan-allergens)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12652208/) [snippet]; [Sci Rep 2020 (BSF allergenicity)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-57863-5)
- A case report documents airborne occupational type I allergy to mealworm (Tenebrio molitor) — [PMC 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12288675/)

### Inferences
- At 1–5 t/day Tryento can expect 1–3 rearing staff plus people for waste handling, harvest, drying and packing. The Eawag Mon–Wed–Fri schedule shows how a small team can run the operation without 7-day presence, at the cost of more variable output.
- Workers with shellfish or dust-mite allergy or asthma are at elevated risk. Practical SOP items:
  - FFP2/N95 masks for dusty tasks: sieving frass, handling dry larvae or meal, emptying love cages of dead flies.
  - Gloves and long sleeves.
  - Local ventilation at sieves and grinders.
  - Baseline and annual symptom questionnaires.
- The PWA could log PPE and training sign-offs per SOP step, as the Eawag SOPs imply.

### Gaps
- No BSF-specific occupational exposure data (dust mg/m³, endotoxin, NH3 ppm at the workplace) was found.
- No data on labour hours per tonne of larvae for tropical small farms.
- Training curricula or certifications for BSF operators in Latin America were not found.

## 10. Sustainability: LCA results, when BSF beats or loses to soy and fishmeal, hotspots

### Takeaway
LCAs consistently find that BSF only beats soy or fishmeal when larvae are reared on **true residues with low opportunity cost** and **low-carbon heat or electricity**, and when credits for avoided waste treatment are counted. On purpose-grown feed with fossil energy, insect protein usually has higher impacts than soybean meal and fishmeal. Tropical sites avoid most heating energy, which is the biggest structural LCA advantage available to a Venezuelan farm.

### Cited Findings
- The environmental hotspots of insect production are "insect feed provision and energy use for insect production and processing", affecting fossil resources, water, land use and climate change. "Soybean meal and fishmeal are less harmful than insect-derived protein in most cases." When by-product feed (potato starch) is combined with renewable or residual heat, insect protein "came closer" to competing. — [Resources, Conservation & Recycling 2023, "BSF larvae as alternative feed and agro-waste disposal – life cycle perspective"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S092134492300054X) [snippet]
- BSFL protein had higher impacts than fishmeal and pea protein but was comparable to soybean meal. It had lower GWP than egg protein, whey concentrate and microalgae. — [search summary of LCA literature, incl. Smetana et al. 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0921344919300515) [snippet]
- BSF systems show "favourable environmental profiles when larvae are reared on low-opportunity-cost organic residues and when credits are given for avoided waste treatment" — [search summary, Sustainability 2025 medium-scale LCA](https://doi.org/10.3390/su17219651) [snippet]
- Salomone et al. (Italy): 1 t of food waste gave about 30 kg of dried larvae and 335 kg of compost, with low global-warming impact per kg of waste treated (about 3.2 kg CO2e per 100 kg waste reported in summaries) — [J. Cleaner Production 140 (2017) 890–905](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959652616308411) [snippet; functional unit not verified]
- InnovaFeed reports 1.2 kg CO2e/kg protein (2025), with an 80% advantage from "industrial symbiosis" (waste heat and co-location) versus a non-symbiotic producer, and a target of 0.6 by 2030. It claims a footprint 50–60% lower than conventional protein alternatives. [CO; white paper co-authored with Protix] — [Food Ingredients First, Sep 2026](https://www.foodingredientsfirst.com/news/insect-protein-scale-carbon-cost.html)
- Eawag published a dedicated global-warming-potential assessment of BSF biowaste treatment — [Eawag GWP PDF](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/bsf_assessment_global_warming.pdf) (not read). A 2023 LCA of biowaste-to-dried-larvae production exists — [F1000Research / PMC10477724](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10477724/) (not read)
- In Eawag's framing, pathogen-risk reduction comes "mainly through material reduction (≈80%) rather than through pathogen inactivation" — [Eawag 2017](https://www.eawag.ch/fileadmin/Domain1/Abteilungen/sandec/publikationen/SWM/BSF/BSF_Biowaste_Processing_LR.pdf)

### Inferences
- For Tryento the LCA story is structurally good if feedstock is genuinely residual (not purchased chicken feed or grain) and energy use is dominated by fans and drying rather than heating. The largest controllable hotspot is likely **drying energy** (LPG or electric), followed by the **nursery or breeding diet** if it uses commercial feed (Eawag's nursery uses chicken feed).
- To make credible sustainability claims later, the data platform should log feed type and source per batch, kWh or LPG per oven run, and fan and heater runtime from the Pi relays. Those are exactly the LCA inventory inputs. Relay on-time multiplied by rated wattage gives a cheap energy estimate.

### Gaps
- No LCA specific to Latin American or tropical BSF farms was found.
- Exact functional units and numbers for Smetana 2019, Bava et al., and Salomone 2017 were not verified (paywalled; snippet-level only).
- No LCA comparing passive tropical rearing against climate-controlled rearing was found.
